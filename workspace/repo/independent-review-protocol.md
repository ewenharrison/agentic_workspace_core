# Constraint-Aware Independent Review Protocol

Use this protocol for independent reviews of grants, manuscripts, programmes, and other competitive submissions. It complements the relevant funder or journal criteria; it does not replace them.

## Purpose

Independent review should identify weaknesses without assuming that every weakness has a viable remedy. A recommendation can improve methodological neatness while weakening fit with the call, ambition, audience, budget, or chosen strategic proposition. Reviews must distinguish a correctable defect from an inherent trade-off.

## Review Sequence

1. Establish the assessment criteria from authoritative sources.
2. Identify the funder's strategic incentives, scale, exclusions, and likely theory of change.
3. State the proposal's central strategic bet before scoring it.
4. Review independently against the criteria. Do not use earlier reviews until the clean-room assessment is complete.
5. Classify each material criticism for actionability.
6. Test each recommendation against the funder's constraints and the proposal's central proposition.
7. Integrate scores, disagreements, residual risks, and genuinely useful next actions.

## Actionability Classes

Every major criticism must be assigned to one of these classes:

- **Directly addressable:** A change can improve the submission without materially changing its central proposition or funder fit.
- **Addressable by clarification or architecture:** The underlying strategy should remain, but its logic, hierarchy, boundaries, hypotheses, or delivery structure can be made clearer.
- **Full-application detail:** The issue is legitimate but premature at the current application stage.
- **Inherent strategic trade-off:** Improvement on one assessment dimension would predictably weaken another dimension valued by the funder.
- **Incompatible recommendation:** The proposed remedy would materially damage call fit, ambition, feasibility, or the proposal's chosen theory of change and should not be presented as an action.

Do not collapse the final three classes into a generic list of weaknesses.

## Counterfactual Test

Before recommending a change, ask:

1. What would the revised proposal become if this recommendation were implemented fully?
2. Would that version be more competitive under the actual call, rather than under a generic grant-review ideal?
3. Would it preserve the scale, ambition, population reach, and paradigm shift sought by the funder?
4. Is there a smaller change that mitigates the concern without surrendering the strategic proposition?

If the answer to question 2 or 3 is no, describe the issue as a trade-off and propose mitigation rather than scope replacement.

## Broad Programme Reviews

For broad programmes, assess these levels separately:

- Programme-level ambition and scope.
- The shared scientific, clinical, or technical spine that makes the breadth coherent.
- Work-package questions and falsifiable hypotheses.
- The bounded intended use, estimand, or decision evaluated at each stage.
- Expansion, transportability, and later deployment roles.

Do not automatically recommend converting a broad programme into a single-condition study. First test whether the funder is explicitly seeking a population-level or system-level transformation. Where breadth is strategically necessary, ask how it can be made tractable through event families, nested hypotheses, staged gates, modular actions, or a focused initial evaluation.

## Multi-Agent Roles

- **Criteria analyst, non-scoring:** Defines authoritative criteria, call incentives, exclusions, stage-specific expectations, and tensions between them.
- **Domain reviewer:** Assesses clinical or substantive importance, coherence, outcomes, and likely benefit.
- **Methods reviewer:** Assesses scientific hypotheses, data, models, comparators, uncertainty, and falsifiability.
- **Delivery reviewer:** Assesses team, partnerships, governance, implementation, regulation, resources, and scale-up.
- **Integrator:** Preserves genuine disagreement, classifies actionability, applies the counterfactual test, and prevents a generic preference from being presented as a compulsory fix.

## Required Output

Include:

1. Criteria and scoring basis.
2. The proposal's strategic bet and its fit with the call.
3. Independent findings and scores.
4. An actionability table:

| Issue | Evidence | Classification | What can change | What should be preserved | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

5. Highest-value actions that preserve funder fit.
6. Strategic tensions that cannot be removed without changing the proposal.
7. Stage-appropriate matters to defer.
8. Likely panel questions.

Scores should reflect the application as submitted. The actionability analysis explains what the applicant can reasonably do; it must not silently inflate the score.

## Guardrails

- Separate non-compliance and feasibility defects from reviewer preference.
- Do not reward methodological simplicity at the expense of an explicitly requested transformative scale.
- Do not assume that a narrower proposal is automatically a better proposal.
- Do not use funder constraints as an excuse for avoidable ambiguity, repetition, or weak causal logic.
- Preserve the independent review as an audit. Add applicant interpretation separately rather than rewriting the reviewers' findings after discussion.
